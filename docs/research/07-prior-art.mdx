NTL's core mechanism — routing weights learned from delivery outcomes — is
**not new**. It has a thirty-year literature, and pretending otherwise would
be both dishonest and strategically foolish: the first question any informed
reader asks is "isn't this just Q-routing?"

It partly is. This document says what NTL takes from its lineage, what it
adds, and where existing systems are simply the better choice.

## Summary

| System | Era | Shares with NTL | NTL differs by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q-routing** | 1994 | Reinforcement-learned routing from delivery feedback | Per-signal-type affinity; runs at the application transport layer, not inside routers |
| **AntNet** | 1998 | Stigmergic reinforcement with pheromone evaporation | Evaporation becomes an explicit half-life; probabilistic selection is specified rather than emergent |
| **Named Data Networking** | 2009– | Content-addressed forwarding; interest-based routing | Signals are typed and weighted, not named; no caching layer |
| **Delay-tolerant networking** | 2003– | Store-and-forward under intermittent connectivity | Learned path selection rather than epidemic or utility heuristics |
| **libp2p** | 2015– | Peer discovery, multi-transport, pluggable crypto | Routing weights are learned from outcomes, not from DHT distance |
| **MQTT** | 1999 | Lightweight pub/sub for constrained devices | Brokerless; no fixed topic hierarchy |
| **NATS** | 2011 | Subject-based routing, at-least-once semantics | Weights adapt per node; no cluster-wide routing table |

## Reinforcement-Learned Routing

### Q-routing (Boyan & Littman, 1994)

The direct ancestor. Each node holds a Q-table estimating delivery time to
each destination via each neighbour, updated from the delivery times actually
observed. Q-routing beats shortest-path routing under congestion, because it
learns that the short path is slow when it is busy.

**NTL takes:** the central claim, which is that routing learned from
observed outcomes outperforms routing computed from static topology.

**NTL differs:**

- Q-routing estimates *delivery time to a destination*. NTL estimates
  *suitability of a synapse for a signal type* — a different and smaller
  learning problem, because most NTL traffic is not addressed to a specific
  destination at all.
- Q-routing lives in the router. NTL lives at the application transport
  layer, so it can see semantics (signal type, tags) that a router cannot.
- Q-routing assumes a cooperative network. NTL assumes an adversarial one,
  which is why it carries influence caps and a
  [threat model](/spec/threat-model) — the literature largely does not,
  because it predates open networks as a deployment target.

**Honest assessment:** if you control your network end to end and only need
to minimise latency to known destinations, Q-routing's formulation is
simpler and better studied. NTL's added machinery earns its keep when the
network is open and the traffic is heterogeneous.

### AntNet (Di Caro & Dorigo, 1998)

Ant-colony routing: probe agents deposit "pheromone" on paths that work, and
pheromone evaporates over time so stale paths fade.

**NTL takes:** two mechanisms almost directly. Weight decay *is* pheromone
evaporation, and stochastic path selection *is* the probabilistic transition
rule. The
[learning-model](/spec/learning-model) says so explicitly rather than
reinventing the vocabulary.

**NTL differs:** AntNet's exploration comes from dedicated probe agents;
NTL explores with real traffic, which is cheaper but means exploration costs
real delivery attempts. That is why NTL's exploration temperature is tunable
and bounded, rather than a fixed probe rate.

### Why This Lineage Was Never Widely Deployed

Learned routing has been known to work since 1994 and is essentially absent
from production networks. Any honest positioning has to engage with why:

1. **Convergence is hard to reason about.** Operators prefer routing they can
   predict and debug over routing that is on average better.
2. **BGP and OSPF were good enough**, and the switching cost of replacing
   working infrastructure is enormous.
3. **No adversarial model.** The academic work assumes cooperative nodes.
   On the open internet, learned weights are an attack surface — which is
   precisely the gap [threat-model](/spec/threat-model) exists to fill.
4. **Nothing to learn from.** Classical routing lacks a per-flow outcome
   signal at the transport layer.

NTL's bet is that (1) and (2) do not apply at the *application* transport
layer — there is no incumbent to displace and no operator expecting
BGP-like determinism — and that (3) and (4) are addressable with signed
receipts and per-identity influence caps. That bet may be wrong. It is at
least a specific bet, rather than an assumption that the idea is new.

## Content-Based Networking

### Named Data Networking / ICN

NDN routes on *content names* rather than host addresses: a consumer
expresses interest in a name, and the network finds it, caching along the
way.

**Parallel:** NTL's `Gradient` scope is close in spirit — signals route
toward nodes that have historically handled that *type*, which is
type-directed rather than address-directed routing.

**Differences:** NDN's names are hierarchical and its caching layer is
central to the design. NTL has neither: signals carry types and tags, not
names, and NTL caches nothing (its dedup cache prevents loops, it does not
serve content). NDN is the better choice for content distribution; NTL is
aimed at signal transport with side effects.

## Delay-Tolerant Networking

Directly relevant to NTL's target deployments. DTN assumes no
contemporaneous end-to-end path exists — intermittent links, long delays,
frequent partition — and uses store-and-forward with custody transfer.

This is the reality of the intermittent-connectivity deployments NTL is
built for, and DTN's literature (Bundle Protocol, RFC 9171; PRoPHET;
Spray-and-Wait) is the most operationally honest body of work in this space.

**NTL takes:** the premise. Partition is normal, not exceptional, which is
why [delivery-semantics](/spec/delivery-semantics) does not pretend to offer
guaranteed delivery and why timeouts are penalised weakly — on an
intermittent link, a timeout is often not a routing failure.

**NTL differs:** DTN forwarding heuristics are mostly hand-designed
(encounter history, delivery predictability). NTL learns them. Whether
learning beats a well-tuned DTN heuristic on a genuinely sparse network is
**an open question, and not one NTL has yet answered with data.**

<Note>
Where connectivity is *very* sparse — days between contacts — DTN's Bundle
Protocol is the mature, correct choice, and NTL has no evidence to offer
against it. NTL targets the intermittent-but-frequent regime: minutes to
hours of disconnection, where enough traffic flows to train on.
</Note>

## Peer-to-Peer and Messaging Systems

### libp2p

The closest thing to a shared substrate. libp2p supplies transport
negotiation, peer discovery (Kademlia DHT), NAT traversal, and pluggable
crypto — much of NTL's synapse and topology layers, already battle-tested.

**Shares:** multi-transport, pluggable crypto, peer identity from public
keys, no central registry.

**Adds:** libp2p's DHT routing is *structural* — it routes by XOR distance
in key space, which is deterministic and ignores whether a peer is actually
good at handling your traffic. NTL's routing is *learned* from outcomes. NTL
also adds activation backpressure and transform-at-synapse, neither of which
libp2p addresses.

**Honest assessment:** NTL could plausibly have been built *on* libp2p
rather than beside it, and a reasonable reviewer will ask why it was not.
The answer is control over the synapse abstraction — libp2p connections are
not weighted, stateful, learning objects — but this is a real trade-off
against a great deal of proven engineering, not a clear win.

### MQTT

The incumbent for constrained devices. Lightweight, broker-based, three QoS
levels, and enormous deployed reach.

**Shares:** the constrained-device target; QoS 1 is NTL's `acknowledged`
class.

**Adds:** MQTT requires a broker, which is a single point of failure and a
deployment prerequisite. NTL is brokerless and peer-to-peer. MQTT's topic
hierarchy must be designed up front; NTL's type affinity is learned.

**Honest assessment:** if a broker is acceptable, **MQTT is the pragmatic
choice** — mature, tiny, and universally supported. NTL is for cases where a
broker is not acceptable: no reliable central infrastructure, or mesh
topologies where "where do I put the broker" has no good answer.

### NATS

Subject-based messaging with clustering, at-least-once delivery via
JetStream, and excellent operational characteristics.

**Shares:** subject/type-based routing, at-least-once semantics, lightweight
wire protocol.

**Adds:** NATS routing within a cluster is configured; NTL's adapts per
node from observed outcomes. NATS assumes a reasonably stable cluster; NTL
assumes churn.

**Honest assessment:** for a datacentre or a stable cloud deployment, NATS
is more mature and almost certainly the better engineering choice today.

## What Is Actually Novel

Stripped of marketing, NTL's genuinely new contribution is **the
combination**, not any single mechanism:

1. **Learned routing at the application transport layer**, with per-signal-type
   affinity. Q-routing learns per destination; NTL learns per type, which
   suits traffic that is mostly not addressed to a specific node.
2. **An adversarial model for learned routing.** The academic literature
   assumes cooperative nodes. Per-identity influence caps, signed reward
   receipts, and an explicit out-of-scope list appear to be new to this
   space, and are the parts most likely to matter in practice.
3. **Activation backpressure as a first-class protocol mechanism.**
   Threshold-and-refractory admission control instead of rate limiting.
4. **Transform-at-synapse.** Data reshaped in transit as a property of the
   connection rather than of an endpoint.
5. **Storage as a specified interface** rather than an assumed database.

Items 2 and 3 are the strongest claims. Item 1 is a modest extension of
existing work. Items 4 and 5 are engineering choices rather than research
contributions.

## What Would Falsify NTL's Thesis

Stated so the project can be held to it:

- **Learned weights fail to beat static configuration** on a realistic
  intermittent network. Then NTL is a worse libp2p with extra machinery.
- **Influence caps prove insufficient** against cheap Sybil identities in
  practice. Then learned routing is unsafe on open networks, and NTL is a
  consortium-only protocol.
- **The exploration cost is too high.** If probing with real traffic
  degrades delivery more than better routing improves it, the model is not
  worth its price.
- **Nobody emits receipts.** Without outcome feedback the model has no
  gradient, and NTL is an ordinary pub/sub system carrying unused
  machinery.

None of these is currently settled by evidence. The
[benchmarks](https://github.com/openNTL/ntl/tree/main/benchmarks) exist to
start settling the first and third.
